For your review, in seven parts

A quieter, heavier way to set the page.

The seven sketches you've been iterating on share an honest, documentary character — and a slightly anonymous one. This memo proposes a single visual vocabulary that keeps the documentary ethos and lends it weight: a literary serif for display, a neutral grotesk for body, and a small set of motifs (corner brackets, five-block anatomy, oxide accent) that show up consistently across the landing, the research page, the positioning page, and the notes essay. Four worked examples follow.

§ I.

What's working, what isn't, three moves.

The current set is restrained in a good way — no unnecessary product chrome, no AI tropes, no decorative gradients. But restraint isn't yet doing identity work. Two columns of diagnosis below, then the three moves that follow from them.

Working

  • Tone is honest. No 'AI-powered' bombast, no SaaS gloss. Reads like a real research project.
  • Hierarchy is sound. Mono + sans pairing reads as 'spec' without trying too hard.
  • Each sketch has a clear job. Landing, research, positioning, notes are correctly differentiated by register.
  • The 5-block anatomy is a real visual asset. Manifest, data, style, root, runtime — that's a logo waiting to happen.

Not yet working

  • Type is anonymous. System sans does the lifting alone; no display face means no voice.
  • Two palettes drift apart. Warm cream + orange in some, cool fog + blue in others — pages don't feel like siblings.
  • The "capsule" metaphor is named but invisible. Nothing on the page reads as sealed, archival, or contained.
  • The essay reads as a long div. The notes file deserves real editorial setting — drop cap, marginalia, pull quotes.

Move 01

Adopt a literary serif for display.

Newsreader (Google Fonts) — has italic, has optical sizes, has scholarly character. Pair with Geist + Geist Mono so body stays clean. The serif is the voice; everything else stays a whisper.

Move 02

Unify on warm paper + oxide.

One paper (#f4f0e6, warm cream) and one accent (#b44518, oxide). The accent reads as wax seal, archival ink, or weathered stamp — not 'product brand color'. Cool fog stays available for the spec/code register but uses the same accent.

Move 03

Promote the capsule motifs.

Corner brackets framing key plates. Five-block anatomy as a recurring panel. Italic ordinals (§I, §II) instead of numerals. Page reads, at a glance, as a sealed document — not a website about sealed documents.

HTML Capsule

An open spec for sealed, self-contained HTML artifacts — and the empirical evidence that the contract holds across producers.

Luke Schuss · Maintainer · Vancouver, BC · lukeschuss.com · github

Abstract

Five required inline blocks, twelve numbered rules, no network dependencies. The central claim is multi-producer interop across LLMs, deterministic compilers, and human authors. Backed by 30+ LLM-emitted capsules, a third-party compiler validating 26/26 conformance from spec prose alone, and a reactive evolution path from v0.1.0 to v0.3.5.

Field notes · spec authorship

What I do with all the HTML I get from AI.

A first-person account of writing down a contract for HTML artifacts — and the year of copy-paste between AI tools that made one feel necessary.

By Luke Schuss · Vancouver, BC · capsule_version 1.2.0 · 4 200 words

§ I Copy-paste
era

Just want to give you my journey here with AI tools. I've been using all the chat systems — ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini — and more recently the CLI and harness forms. For the longest time I was doing work in each, then copying and pasting in and out, trying to reliably get my info out of these systems into a format I could archive or share. It was a mess.

The share buttons exist, but something doesn't sit right. The chats get lost, or I'm not confident what version they show. Are they still updating? Will the link still work next month? I could dig into the documentation — but will the documentation be the same next month either?

The .md files were portable. The experience wasn't. The themes, the plugins, the rendered output all lived in the app, not the file.
§ II The HTML
pivot

Around then I came across Steph Ango's File over App. The principle is right — your work should live in files you control, in formats that outlive any specific app. But my struggle with Obsidian was the same principle taken one step further than the essay intends. The file was portable; the experience wasn't.

Putting things into HTML has been a huge shift. Even with chat conversations I was always trying to get the data out — asking for a markdown summary, a PDF, copying into Docs. Nothing ever looked right. So I stopped converting. I started keeping what the LLM gave me as HTML, then writing down what HTML would need to be to be trustworthy as an artifact.

End of memo

One vocabulary, four registers — seven pages that read as siblings.

The same kit (Newsreader display, Geist body, oxide accent, corner brackets, five-block anatomy) applied across the front-door, the research note, the position paper, and the essay. The remaining three files — index.html (the exploration page), landing-sketch.html, and research-sketch.html — collapse naturally into the v2 mockups above. Happy to go deeper on any of these, or take the kit somewhere different.
