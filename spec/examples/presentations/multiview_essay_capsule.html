Quiet Notes on HTML

A long-form note fixture for testing whether continuous reader surfaces remain necessary when snap, feed, and story views also exist.

The premise

HTML is useful as an interchange layer because it can carry prose, structure, styling, and interaction in one ordinary file. That does not mean every artifact should become an app. The strongest default is still a readable document.

The reader layer

A long-form capsule should let a person settle into the text. Headings, paragraphs, quotations, and citations matter more than clever navigation. The mobile version should respect that slower reading posture.

The alternate views

Snap, feed, slides, and story can still be useful, but they should be derived from the essay rather than replacing it. Their job is orientation and review, not the only path to the meaning.

The constraint

If the reader layer becomes too thin, the Capsule loses its archival center. A future LLM, a browser with scripts disabled, or a careful human reader should all be able to understand the work from static HTML.

The conclusion

Long-form content keeps the normal reader style as its default. Other mobile styles can exist, but the compiler should not make the artifact feel busier than the writing requires.
